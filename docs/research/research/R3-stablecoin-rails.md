## Rail comparison (B2B view, typical US or cross-border values)

| Rail | Cost | Speed | Finality | Reversibility | Compliance load | B2B fit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Card (incl. virtual and commercial cards) | High. About 1.5 to 3.5 percent, plus FX markup | Auth in seconds, settlement T+1 to T+2 | Weak. Chargeback window up to 120 days or more | High through chargebacks | Low for the payer. Network and acquirer carry it | Good for spend and small suppliers. Poor for large invoices |
| ACH or wire (incl. SWIFT) | ACH low, cents. Wire 15 to 50 USD, cross-border plus FX and correspondent fees | ACH same day to T+2. Cross-border wire 1 to 5 days | Wire final on receipt. ACH returns possible | ACH returns up to 2 days (60 for consumers). Wire hard to recall | Mature bank KYC, sanctions screening by banks | Default AP and AR rail. Slow and costly cross-border |
| RTP or FedNow | Low, cents per payment | Seconds, 24/7 | Final and irrevocable | None, request for return only | Bank level, same as ACH | Strong for domestic US urgent payments. No cross-border, value caps apply |
| Stablecoin (USDC, PYUSD, USDT on Arc, Tempo, Solana, Ethereum L2) | Low on chain, cents. Ramp fees 0.1 to 1 percent | Seconds to minutes, 24/7, global | Final on chain after block confirmation | None at protocol level. Issuer can freeze | High for the user. Travel Rule, wallet screening, GENIUS and MiCA status, tax and accounting | Strong for cross-border supplier payouts, treasury moves between entities, emerging market corridors |
| Tokenized deposit (JPMD, future TCH network) | Low inside the network. Bank pricing | Seconds, 24/7 within member banks | Final on bank ledger | Bank can reverse under its rules | Lowest new load. Stays a bank deposit under bank KYC | Strong for large corporates already at member banks. Limited reach outside the network |

## Five architecture implications

1. Build a rail router, not a stablecoin product. A single payment intent should pick card, ACH, RTP, stablecoin or tokenized deposit by cost, speed, corridor and counterparty readiness, with policy rules the treasurer can read.
2. Treat compliance as its own service in the flow. Wallet screening, Travel Rule message exchange and sanctions checks must run before any on chain send, since finality means there is no undo. Plan for the coming OCC and Treasury BSA, AML and OFAC rule to change the inputs.
3. Keep issuer and chain choice behind an adapter. USDC on Arc via CPN, USDC or PYUSD via Stripe Bridge on Tempo, and bank deposit tokens will all coexist. Rule level details such as wrapped token status and rewards limits will shift until 2027-01-18 and after.
4. Model the ramp as the real cost and risk point. Most value and fees sit in fiat to coin and coin to fiat conversion, so integrate more than one ramp per corridor, track ramp licences (MiCA CASP, US money transmitter or GENIUS issuer) and keep fallback to wire.
5. Reconcile on chain events into the ERP as first class records. Each transfer needs an invoice reference, legal entity, FX rate and proof of screening attached, so audit, tax and treasury see the same truth as the ledger. Treasury agents can then sweep idle balances on a rule set, not by hand.
